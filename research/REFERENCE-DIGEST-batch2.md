## The blueprint (as the article states it)

Sub-avatar first (one very specific person + their private fear) → ordinary world (seconds) →
humiliation → wound deepens → hero freezes → hero agrees with the insult → failed solutions → rock
bottom → a close, credentialed mentor who is the "complete version" of the hero → the gift (truth
reframe + product) → doubt → week-by-week climb others notice → vindication in the same place, in
front of the same people → short pitch at the emotional peak. Pain always comes through other
characters' dialogue, never the hero's self-description.

## What the videos show that the article doesn't

**Resilia (Andre, 47):** Pixar-style 3D, sung/rap-paced, cuts every ~2–3s, burned-in captions, gold
"WEEK ONE" title cards for the climb, ends on a screen recording of the real landing page
(30-day guarantee). Character consistency drifts badly (Andre changes face/body shape between shots)
and it doesn't seem to matter to the story. Claims are aggressive ("clinically proven", "sell out",
"small batches", "70% off" pattern).

**Ori's Opus 5.5 ad (a wife cropped out of the family photo):** this is a *reconciliation* story, not
revenge — proof the blueprint works in a gentler register. Its craft is the best thing in batch 2:
- **Bookend artifact.** Opens on an Instagram post with her cropped out; ends on a new post with her
  in it (342 → 1,204 likes). The whole arc is told by one object changing.
- **A refrain that returns with new meaning.** "Stand still, baby, let me get one of you" — first as
  memory of how he used to see her, last at the graduation when he shoos the photographer away.
- **Every line is a picture.** "I'm the arm at the edge of the frame." "Two blankets and a TV glow."
  "Sock lines pressed in my ankles by noon." "My ring stopped… I took it off in March. He saw my bare
  hand at dinner. He didn't say one word." "One tab still open: one-bedroom apartments, ten minutes
  from his job." Then the reversals: "ring slid on", "apartment tab was closed", "two blankets turned
  to one".
- **Pain voiced by others:** "It's like living with my sister… I'm staying for the kids" (overheard).
- Visually weaker than Resilia: mostly still-looking illustrated frames, yellow keyword captions.

## For Waypoint: keep / adapt / reject

**Keep**
- **Sub-avatar as the engine's main input.** Kelsey's avatar (40–60, M/F, corporate refugee) splits
  into sub-avatars, e.g. a 52-year-old ops VP asked to train their replacement; a 46-year-old woman
  cut in a restructure after 18 years; a 58-year-old engineer whose company got acquired. Change the
  sub-avatar and every beat changes. This is how one story engine produces many ads.
- **Bookend artifact + returning refrain.** The Unfinished Song already has both (the song, "next
  weekend") — Ori's ad shows how hard to lean on them.
- **Concrete observable details instead of summary.** Adopt as a script rule: no line that can't be
  drawn.
- **Pain through other people's words.**
- **Doubt voiced by the hero** = the viewer's objections ("I'm too old", "I don't have that kind of
  money", "franchises are a scam").
- **Failed solutions** map perfectly to our audience: 60 job applications, LinkedIn rewrites, a
  side-hustle course, a franchise expo with 100 brands and pushy sellers.

**Adapt**
- **Mentor → someone close who went through the exploration**, and the "gift" is a reframe + a next
  step (the assessment / a conversation), not a miracle product.
- **The climb → steps of clarity, not results.** Week 1 the assessment, week 2 the first call, week 3
  learning what "fit" means… others notice they're lighter, not richer.
- **Vindication → return to the same place, changed by a decision**, never by money. No revenge.

**Reject**
- Betrayal-by-spouse/best-friend humiliation and revenge payoffs (off-brand for Waypoint's calm
  authority, and the ChatGPT evaluation agreed).
- Scarcity, discount, "clinically proven"-style claims and any earnings or outcome promise.
- 7–10 minute length as a default (untested for us; our default stays ~60s, 9:16 / 1:1).
