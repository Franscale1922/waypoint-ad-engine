## Why this shape
Emotional story first, message second (Kelsey, 2026-09-29). The first 45 seconds are a father and
daughter and nothing else. The song gets a real scene (about a minute), because finishing it is what
the opening line promised. Business first appears at about 1:45, and it arrives as an object in his
own suitcase, not as a narrator's turn: every earlier version broke when a line had to jump from
family to franchising. With room to run, the pictures make that jump instead.

## The average, refused (rule 1)
The obvious dad-and-daughter regret ad: tears, a slow-motion hug, sad piano, "time is the one thing
you can't get back", "it's never too late", then "start your franchise journey" over a sunrise and a
storefront. **Banned for this piece:** tears, the hug, piano (they play guitar), any promise or hint
that a business gives time or family back, "never too late", "time you can't get back", "journey",
a narrator who moralises about fatherhood.
**The back half's average, also banned (v9):** the late-night laptop glowing with the quiz, the
resignation letter, quitting in a grand gesture, a missed flight as the sign he changed, the song
played perfectly. He still flies; the song is a little bad; the step is small.
**The photoreal average, also banned (v8):** glossy poreless skin, golden-hour backlight, shallow
focus on everything, teal-and-orange grade, a beige living room, slow motion, a symmetrical centred
two-shot, Pixar-perfect faces. Instead: real skin, a lived-in room, the floor lamp as the only light
in the night scenes, a grey room with one colour that has a job (the crayon).

## Stance
**2. Your employer isn't the villain. Your waiting is.** "Next weekend" is the villain of the whole
ad: Chloe says it, Arthur nearly says it again and stops, the brochure says NOW in 2019, the legal
pad's years are crossed out four times, and the CTA ends it: "Not next weekend." He never quits or
blames his job; he still gets on the plane. Where a competitor flinches: a broker's ad sells the
leap. This one sells naming a day.
(v8 closed on stance 1, "Most people shouldn't buy a franchise". Kelsey's CTA replaced it; stance 2
carries the ad on its own.)
